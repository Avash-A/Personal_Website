SENIOR DESIGN

Renewable Energy Power Conversion with GaN Device

Project Overview

This senior design project focuses on developing a small-scale renewable-energy power system that integrates solar and wind generation, battery storage, DC-DC power conversion, embedded control, sensing, and semiconductor-device development.

The system is being designed so renewable sources charge a battery, while a controlled power-conversion stage delivers regulated power to representative electrical loads. The project also includes the fabrication and characterization of a GaN power semiconductor device intended to eventually replace the commercial switching device used in the converter.

Project Objectives

  • Integrate solar and wind power sources into a common renewable-energy system.
  • Charge and operate a battery-based energy-storage stage safely.
  • Design and simulate DC-DC power-conversion circuitry.
  • Generate converter PWM control using an Arduino microcontroller.
  • Implement voltage/current sensing for system monitoring and control.
  • Design appropriate gate-drive circuitry for the switching device.
  • Fabricate and characterize a GaN power semiconductor device.
  • Replace the commercial converter switch with the fabricated GaN device in a later project phase.

Solar & Wind Generation

The renewable source model combines solar and wind generation to provide energy to the battery-storage system. The project uses multiple small solar panels and wind-generator models to represent hybrid renewable generation in a laboratory-scale demonstration.

Combined System Target

24 W at approximately 12 V and 2 A

Solar Subsystem

Approximately 12 W

Wind Subsystem

Approximately 12 W

Wind Generation Concept

Multiple small generators mechanically driven through a common external motor and belt arrangement for the physical demonstration model.

These values are design targets for the laboratory model and do not represent measured final performance.

Battery Storage

The energy-storage stage uses a 12 V LiFePO4 battery with an integrated battery-management system (BMS). The battery provides a stable energy source between the intermittent renewable generation stage and the downstream power converter.

  • 12 V LiFePO4 battery
  • approximately 5–6 Ah capacity
  • integrated BMS
  • over-voltage / under-voltage protection
  • short-circuit protection
  • renewable-energy buffering

Power Conversion

The project investigates DC-DC converter stages for controlling renewable-energy charging and providing regulated power from the battery. LTspice is used to design and evaluate the power stage before physical implementation.

The converter architecture includes development before and/or after the battery as the system design evolves.

Boost Converter / Charging Stage

Converter Elements

  • inductor-based DC-DC boost topology
  • switching MOSFET / GaN device
  • Schottky diode
  • input and output capacitors
  • gate-drive circuit
  • current and voltage sensing
  • PWM duty-cycle control

Arduino Control & PWM

An Arduino microcontroller is used as the control and monitoring platform for the converter system. It generates PWM signals for converter switching and reads voltage/current sensors throughout the renewable-energy and load paths.

  • PWM generation
  • duty-cycle adjustment
  • voltage sensing
  • current sensing
  • converter-control logic
  • monitoring of renewable input and load branches

One Arduino is being investigated for controlling multiple converter stages and several sensors. A completed closed-loop controller is not assumed at this stage.

Gate-Drive Circuitry

Because the Arduino cannot directly drive a power transistor efficiently, a dedicated gate-driver IC is used between the microcontroller and the converter switch. The driver provides the current and switching capability required to charge and discharge the transistor gate quickly.

  • Arduino PWM input
  • gate-driver IC
  • gate resistor
  • gate-source pull-down resistor
  • local decoupling capacitors
  • power MOSFET / GaN gate

Sensing & Monitoring

The project monitors important electrical points throughout the system. Intended sensing points include:

  • combined wind generation
  • combined solar generation
  • combined renewable-energy bus
  • converter output
  • battery output
  • individual load branches

Voltage/current measurements are intended to support system monitoring and future feedback-control implementation.

GaN Power Semiconductor Device

A major goal of the project is to transition from a converter built with a commercial switching device to one that uses a GaN power semiconductor device fabricated and characterized as part of the project.

Phase 1

Build, simulate, and demonstrate the renewable-energy power-conversion system using commercially available switching devices.

Phase 2

Fabricate and characterize a GaN power device and evaluate its use as the switching element in the previously developed converter.

The fabrication work is intended to connect semiconductor-device engineering directly with a practical power-electronics application.
