RESEARCH

TCAD Investigation of Ferroelectric Semiconductor Devices

My undergraduate research focuses on using TCAD simulation to investigate the behavior of ferroelectric semiconductor devices, with particular interest in AlScN-based transistor structures, polarization-dependent transport, hysteresis, and potential nonvolatile-memory and in-memory-computing applications.

Research Overview

Ferroelectric materials are typically investigated as dielectric or gate-stack layers. In this work, I explored the possibility of using an AlScN-like ferroelectric semiconductor as an active transistor channel that can both conduct current and retain polarization-dependent states.

The research uses device-level TCAD simulation to study transistor transfer and output characteristics and to evaluate how ferroelectric behavior, doping, geometry, interface charge, and other device parameters affect electrical response.

Research Objectives

  • Investigate whether a ferroelectric semiconductor channel can exhibit transistor-like conduction.
  • Study polarization-dependent electrical behavior.
  • Analyze hysteresis between forward and reverse voltage sweeps.
  • Evaluate the influence of doping concentration.
  • Evaluate the influence of device thickness.
  • Investigate interface-charge effects.
  • Study ferroelectric parameters such as remanent polarization, saturation polarization, coercive field, and dielectric response.
  • Explore potential relevance to nonvolatile memory and in-memory computing.

Device Concept

The research investigates MOSFET-like structures in which the ferroelectric semiconductor material is treated as an active current-carrying region rather than only as a gate dielectric.

Key Observations

  • The simulated devices exhibited MOSFET-like transfer and output behavior.
  • Forward and reverse voltage sweeps produced polarization-dependent hysteresis under the ferroelectric model.
  • Device characteristics changed with doping, geometry, interface charge, and ferroelectric parameters.
  • The combined conduction and polarization-dependent response suggests potential relevance to memory-oriented device concepts.

Why This Research Matters

Most AlScN ferroelectric-device research focuses on using the material as a dielectric or gate-stack layer. This project explores a different concept: whether a ferroelectric semiconductor channel could combine current conduction and polarization-state retention in the same active region.

This type of behavior could be relevant to future nonvolatile-memory and in-memory-computing concepts, where storage and computation are more closely integrated.

Current Status

This work is currently simulation-based. TCAD modeling and parameter studies have produced promising transistor-like and polarization-dependent characteristics. Experimental fabrication and measurement have not yet been performed.
